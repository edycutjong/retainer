hedera schedule service · HIP-1215 · 0x16b

Three methods, all load‑bearing.

scheduleCall(this, expiresAt, gas, renew(agent))
Arms the next renewal. This is the product. Without it: a cron job.
hasScheduleCapacity(expiresAt, gas)
Asked before arming — a full second lapses visibly instead of dying silently.
deleteSchedule(pending)
cancel() releases the booked call; the gas comes back.

Metered, not flat.

Calls are counted on-chain by meter(agent); the renewal refills the meter.

Lapses loudly.

Always a reason, never silence: "balance will not cover the next period"

And stays lapsed.

Nothing re-arms itself: renew() reverts NotSubscribed(), fund() only adds money. Subscribing again is the way back.

retainer.edycu.devv1.2.0
Say: "Remove any one of these three calls and the product breaks rather than degrades. scheduleCall is the renewal itself. hasScheduleCapacity is why a full network second is a visible event instead of a dead subscription. deleteSchedule is why cancelling gives the gas back." If asked about depth: "That is three Schedule Service methods doing real work, plus the metering write and the x402 exact scheme — the SDK is the engine, not a sticker." If asked where it stops: "Unattended until the money stops — the subscriber's balance or the seller's gas reserve. After a lapse nothing on-chain is holding a wake-up call, so someone has to subscribe again; renew() reverts NotSubscribed from there. At demo settings — 90-second periods, 2 HBAR held back per armed renewal — that is minutes."

measured on hedera testnet · mirror node

Re-arming is 97% of the cost.

renewal that re-arms the nextrenew() + scheduleCall into 0x16b
1.54896 ℏ154,896,000 tinybar
renewal that does not re-armrenew() then Lapsed — no scheduleCall
0.0507 ℏ5,067,825 tinybar · ~30× less

Same function, executed twice by the network. The only difference: one called scheduleCall. Buying the next wake-up is the price. The live deployment repeats it: 1.5404 vs 0.0522 ℏ.

At 1 ℏ a period, Retainer loses money on every renewal. Break-even ≈ 1.55 ℏ a period — one setTerms() call by the seller, no redeploy. Written up in docs/gas-economics.md.

retainer.edycu.devv1.2.0
Say: "The renewal that re-armed cost 1.549 HBAR. The one that lapsed and did not re-arm cost 0.05. Thirty times. So the interesting cost is not running a subscription — it is buying the next wake-up, one at a time, in advance." Own it: "At the demo price this loses money on every renewal. The fix is pricing a period above the wake-up cost — setTerms is seller-only and needs no redeploy — and right-sizing the gas limit, because Hedera refunds at most 20% of unused gas. We measured it rather than solved it, and we would rather show you the number."
